7 Best AI Meal Planning and Nutrition Tools in 2026 (Eat Better, Plan Less)

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read · Category: Health & Lifestyle

Most people do not fail at healthy eating because they lack willpower — they fail because dinner is unplanned at 6pm. AI meal planning tools fix the planning part: they build a week of meals around your calorie target, diet and budget, generate the grocery list, and adjust when life gets in the way. Here are seven of the best, from fully automatic planners to trackers with smart suggestions.

What to look for in an AI meal planning tool

  • Automation vs. control: Some tools generate the whole week for you; others suggest recipes and let you choose.
  • Diet and allergy support: Vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, halal and allergy filters matter more than fancy features.
  • Grocery list quality: A plan that wastes food or needs three store trips will not survive week two.
  • Tracking: If you want to watch calories or macros, a planner with built-in tracking saves a second app.

Note: these tools offer general nutrition information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, diabetes, an eating disorder or are pregnant, talk to a doctor or dietitian before changing your diet.

1. Eat This Much

Eat This Much is the most automatic planner here: enter your calories, diet, budget and foods you dislike, and it generates a full day or week of meals with portions, nutrition and a grocery list. It is ideal if you want to stop deciding what to eat and just follow a plan. The free version builds a day at a time; the premium version unlocks full weeks, pantry tracking and grocery delivery integration. Drawback: recipes are functional rather than inspiring, and the interface feels dated next to newer apps.

2. Mealime

Mealime is built for busy weeknights: pick your meals for the week, and it builds a grocery list grouped by store section, with most recipes ready in about 30 minutes. Its plans handle common diets and dislikes well, and the cooking mode keeps instructions readable while you cook. Drawback: it focuses on dinners and a few lunches, so breakfast and snacks need another plan. The core app is free, with a pro tier adding more recipes and nutrition detail — check current pricing.

3. Lifesum

Lifesum blends meal plans with calorie and macro tracking, plus a barcode scanner and a food diary that many people find easier to stick with than a spreadsheet-style tracker. Its AI suggestions adapt plans to your goal — weight loss, maintenance or muscle gain — and it scores meals so you can see at a glance whether a choice helps or hurts. Drawback: the best plans and AI features sit behind the premium subscription, and the free tier is mainly a tracker. Premium is usually around $10 per month on annual billing — check current pricing.

4. MyFitnessPal

MyFitnessPal is the tracking giant: the largest food database here, barcode scanning, and AI-assisted logging that learns your frequent meals. Recent versions add meal-plan suggestions and smarter logging, so it now works as both tracker and planner. If you already log food anywhere, this is the easiest switch. Drawback: the free tier has become more limited over time, with barcode scanning and deeper insights moving to premium. Premium is usually around $20 per month, less on annual billing — check current pricing.

5. PlateJoy

PlateJoy is the personalization pick: a detailed quiz covers your household, schedule, diet, health goals and even the appliances you own, then builds a custom plan with a grocery list and optional grocery delivery. It is especially good for families and for people with multiple dietary restrictions under one roof. Drawback: it is subscription-only with no useful free tier, and it is strongest in the US where grocery delivery integrations are widest. Pricing is usually around $10–15 per month on longer plans.

6. Yummly

Yummly is the recipe-discovery tool with AI recommendations: tell it your tastes, diets and dislikes, and it suggests recipes from across the web, with guided cooking, ingredient search and a shopping list. It is best if you enjoy cooking and want variety, rather than a rigid plan. Drawback: it is a recipe app first — it will not set your calories or track your macros unless you pair it with a tracker. The basic app is free, with a premium tier for guided cooking and more features.

7. Samsung Food

Samsung Food (formerly Whisk) turns saved recipes into plans: it imports recipes from the web, builds meal plans and grocery lists, and uses AI to suggest recipes based on what you already have in the pantry. It works on any phone, not just Samsung devices, and the free tier is generous. Drawback: nutrition tracking is lighter than MyFitnessPal or Lifesum, and AI suggestions improve only once you have saved enough recipes. Premium adds AI recipe personalization and more planning features.

Which one should you pick?

  • Just tell me what to eat: Eat This Much — the most automatic plan here.
  • Busy family dinners: Mealime for fast weeknight plans, PlateJoy for a fully personalized household plan.
  • Tracking calories or macros: MyFitnessPal for the biggest database, Lifesum for friendlier guidance.
  • You enjoy cooking: Yummly for recipe discovery, Samsung Food for turning saved recipes into plans.
  • Free to start: Mealime, Yummly or Samsung Food — all useful without paying.

Whichever you choose, give it two full weeks before judging it. The first week's grocery list always feels long; by week two, the pantry, leftovers and your preferences start working in your favour.

FAQ

Can AI meal planners handle allergies and dietary restrictions?

Yes, up to a point — all the tools here filter common diets and dislikes. For serious allergies, always read ingredient labels yourself; an app's filter is not a safety guarantee.

Do I need to follow the plan exactly?

No. Swap meals freely — the plan is a starting point, not a prescription. Most tools recalculate your grocery list and nutrition when you swap a meal.

Which AI meal planner is best for weight loss?

One that combines a plan with tracking: Lifesum or MyFitnessPal, since they show calories and macros against your goal. A plan you never track is easy to quietly overeat.

Are these tools suitable for vegetarians or vegans?

Yes — Eat This Much, Mealime, PlateJoy and Yummly all support vegetarian and vegan plans, and most handle gluten-free and dairy-free filters too.

Can AI meal planning save money?

Often, yes: planned grocery lists cut impulse buys and food waste. The biggest savings come from cooking at home more often, not from the app itself.
