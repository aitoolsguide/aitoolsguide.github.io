7 Best AI Tools for Therapists in 2026 (Notes, Scheduling & Practice Compared)

Updated November 2026 · 9 min read · Category: Healthcare

Therapists spend hours every week on work that is not therapy: writing progress notes, updating treatment plans, and scheduling. AI tools built for mental health practice can take that admin off your plate — drafting notes from sessions, tracking client progress, and keeping your diary organised. This guide compares seven options — Upheal, Blueprint, Eleos, Freed AI, Heidi Health, Nabla, and Mentalyc — so you can choose the one that fits your practice.

Important: AI-generated notes are drafts, not records. Always review, edit, and approve every note yourself before filing it — you are responsible for the client file.

What to look for in an AI tool for therapists

  • HIPAA compliance and privacy: The tool must sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA), encrypt data, and never train models on your sessions. No BAA, no client work.
  • Clinical accuracy: Notes must capture what actually happened — interventions, client response, risk factors — not invent details. Prefer therapy-specific models.
  • Note formats you actually use: Check it supports your format — SOAP, DAP, or BIRP — and generates treatment plans, not just transcripts.
  • Workflow fit: It should work with your EHR, support telehealth and in-person sessions, and add no new admin.
  • Control and transparency: You must be able to review and edit every note before filing and delete recordings easily.

1. Upheal

Upheal is an AI assistant built for mental health professionals: it records sessions, transcribes them, and generates progress notes, treatment plans, and summaries in your preferred format. Best for solo therapists and small practices who want notes that sound like therapy notes. Pricing varies by plan — check current pricing; free trial available.

Its templates are designed around therapy, and it supports in-person, telehealth, and dictation workflows. If notes after every client are your main pain, Upheal is a direct fix.

2. Blueprint

Blueprint pairs AI note-taking with measurement-based care: it drafts progress notes and sends standardised outcome measures to clients between appointments, tracking scores so you can see if treatment is working. Best for therapists who want evidence of progress. Pricing is per clinician — check current pricing.

The measurement side is the standout: charts show how a client's scores change week to week, making supervision and treatment reviews much easier. Setup takes longer, but the data is worth it.

3. Eleos

Eleos is an AI platform for behavioural health organisations: it captures sessions, generates compliant documentation, and analyses conversations for insights about techniques and engagement. Best for group practices, clinics, and agencies needing consistent, audit-ready documentation. Pricing is custom — check current pricing.

Eleos is built for scale, not solo use: it helps supervisors review sessions and flags documentation gaps early. For a large team that consistency is valuable; for a solo therapist it is too much platform.

4. Freed AI

Freed AI is an ambient AI scribe that listens during a session and produces a structured SOAP note in seconds, learning your style over time. Therapists use it widely for fast, clean notes. Best for clinicians who want the quickest path from session to finished note. Subscription around $99/month — check current pricing; free trial available.

Freed's strength is speed: almost nothing to configure, and notes arrive ready to review within a minute of the session ending. As a general medical scribe, it may need template tweaks to match your progress-note style.

5. Heidi Health

Heidi Health is an AI medical scribe that transcribes consultations and generates notes, referral letters, and documents from customisable templates. Therapists like it because it is genuinely usable on a free tier. Best for therapists on a tight budget, or anyone wanting to try AI notes without a subscription. Free tier available.

Templates are flexible — SOAP, DAP, or a custom format — and it works well for telehealth. The free tier covers a small caseload, making it the lowest-risk way to test AI scribing.

6. Nabla

Nabla is an ambient AI assistant that listens to consultations and generates structured clinical notes, with dictation, templates, and EHR integrations. It is used across healthcare, including by therapists who value its clean notes and strong privacy posture. Best for clinicians who want a polished, privacy-conscious scribe. Free tier available.

Nabla is known for note quality and clear data-handling policies, with easy deletion of recordings. Less therapy-specific than Upheal or Mentalyc, but a strong all-rounder.

7. Mentalyc

Mentalyc is an AI note-taker designed specifically for psychotherapists: it records sessions and produces progress notes in DAP, BIRP, SOAP, and other therapy formats, plus treatment plans. Best for psychotherapists who want notes in the language of therapy, with the right structure from the start. Pricing varies by plan — check current pricing.

Built only for therapy, its notes usually need less editing than general scribes — it understands interventions and the difference between a progress note and a transcript.

Which one should you pick?

  • Solo therapist, notes are the bottleneck: Upheal or Mentalyc — both write notes in therapy language; Mentalyc is the more specialised.
  • You need to show client progress with data: Blueprint — its outcome measures turn "I think they're improving" into evidence.
  • You run a clinic or group practice: Eleos — built for consistent documentation and supervision across a team.
  • You want the fastest, simplest scribe: Freed AI — session to finished note in about a minute, with minimal setup.
  • On a tight budget, or just testing AI notes: Heidi Health or Nabla — both have usable free tiers, so try before you pay.

Start with a free trial on a few real sessions, and review every note against what you would have written before adopting it.

FAQ

Are AI tools for therapists HIPAA compliant?

The reputable ones are — but compliance is not automatic. Check the vendor signs a Business Associate Agreement (BAA), encrypts your data, and does not train models on session recordings. No BAA means no client work.

Can AI write my therapy notes for me?

AI can draft them, but it cannot finalise them. Every note must be reviewed, corrected, and approved by you before filing — you remain responsible for the record.

Do clients need to consent to AI note-taking?

Yes. Tell clients sessions may be recorded or transcribed by an AI tool, explain how their data is stored and protected, and get written consent in your intake paperwork.

Will AI notes work for telehealth sessions?

Yes — all seven tools here support telehealth, capturing audio from your video call. Some also support in-person sessions via a phone or laptop microphone.

Which AI tool for therapists is free?

Heidi Health and Nabla both offer usable free tiers, and most others offer free trials. Free tiers usually limit sessions per month, so they suit small caseloads — full practices need a paid plan.
