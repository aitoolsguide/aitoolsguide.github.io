7 Best AI Tools for Event Planners in 2026 (Planning, Networking & Seating)

Updated November 2026 · 9 min read · Category: Business

Event planning is a hundred small jobs wearing one deadline: registrations, seating charts, speaker schedules, sponsor commitments, and endless emails asking "where do I check in?" AI tools now handle the repetitive middle — matching attendees for networking, building seating plans in minutes, and drafting event copy. Here are seven tools worth a serious look, whether you run conferences, weddings, trade shows, or corporate offsites.

What to look for in an AI event-planning tool

  • Your event type: A conference platform is overkill for a wedding, and a wedding tool will not run a 2,000-person trade show. Match the tool to the event.
  • Attendee experience: Registration, the event app, and check-in are the parts guests actually touch — if those feel clunky, no back-end feature saves you.
  • Time saved, not features added: The best tools remove work: automated seating, smart networking matches, and instant answers to common questions.
  • Real costs: Watch for per-attendee fees, per-event pricing, and add-ons for the event app, badge printing, or analytics.

1. Whova

Whova is the all-rounder for conferences and trade shows: registration, an event app with agendas and speaker profiles, live polls, and AI-powered networking that suggests people each attendee should meet. Drawback: the feature set is broad, so small events may pay for tools they never use, and the app setup takes real time. Pricing is typically per event, scaled to attendee numbers — request a quote for your size.

2. Cvent

Cvent is the enterprise workhorse: registration, venue sourcing, attendee management, surveys, and reporting in one platform, with AI features that help draft event websites, summarize survey feedback, and spot registration trends. It handles complex events — multi-track conferences, hotel room blocks, and strict approval workflows — without spreadsheets falling apart. Drawback: it is priced and built for bigger operations; a small planner will find it heavy and expensive. Pricing is quoted per organization, so expect an enterprise conversation.

3. Brella

Brella is built around one thing: networking that actually happens. Its AI matches attendees by interests, goals, and job roles, then lets them book meetings before the event starts. For conferences and B2B events where attendees come to meet people, this turns a vague "networking session" into a calendar full of real conversations. Drawback: it is a networking layer, not a full planning suite — you will still need a registration and event-app tool alongside it. Pricing is usually per event.

4. Social Tables

Social Tables solves the job every planner dreads: seating charts. Upload your floor plan, drag tables into place, and let its tools auto-seat guests by group or dietary needs, then adjust by hand where judgment matters. It also produces check-in lists and printed seating charts, so the plan survives contact with the venue. Drawback: it focuses on diagrams and seating — it will not run registration or ticketing. Pricing is typically a monthly subscription with event-based options.

5. Grip

Grip is another networking specialist, popular at trade shows and expos: its AI recommends exhibitors, sessions, and people to each attendee, and its meeting scheduler turns those recommendations into booked appointments. Organizers get analytics on which exhibitors and sessions drew real interest — gold for sponsor reports. Drawback: like Brella, it complements rather than replaces your main event platform, and its value depends on attendees actually using the app. Pricing is quoted per event.

6. Zkipster

Zkipster handles the front door: guest lists, invitations, RSVPs, and fast check-in with seating and badge tools. Its strength is guest experience at galas, launches, and VIP events — hosts see arrivals in real time, staff can flag VIPs, and no one waits in a clipboard line. It also helps with seating plans and post-event reporting. Drawback: it is less suited to open ticket sales or complex multi-day conferences than Whova or Cvent. Pricing is typically per event or monthly.

7. Planning Pod

Planning Pod is the back-office organizer for planners who juggle many clients: event timelines, task lists, budgets, floor plans, and client collaboration in one place, with templates for weddings, corporate events, and parties. Its AI-assisted drafting helps with proposals and timelines, and clients can approve details online instead of by email thread. Drawback: it is a planning and business tool, not an attendee-facing app — guests will not use it at the event. Pricing is usually a monthly subscription per user.

Which one should you pick?

  • Running a conference or trade show: Whova for the all-in-one platform; add Brella or Grip if networking is the main draw.
  • Large corporate or multi-day events: Cvent — it handles complexity that smaller tools cannot.
  • Seated dinners, weddings, or galas: Social Tables for seating, Zkipster for guest lists and check-in.
  • Independent planner with many clients: Planning Pod to keep timelines, budgets, and clients in one place.
  • Networking-first event: Brella or Grip — attendees leave with meetings booked, not just business cards.

Start with the bottleneck that hurts most — usually registration chaos, seating, or dead networking — and fix that first. One tool that removes a real pain beats a platform whose features you never open.

FAQ

Can AI really match attendees for networking?

Yes, within limits. Tools like Brella and Grip match on stated interests, roles, and goals, and they work best when attendees complete their profiles. They cannot replace chemistry, but they reliably produce more relevant meetings than random mingling.

Do I need a separate tool for seating plans?

For seated events, yes — dedicated tools like Social Tables handle table shapes, guest groups, and last-minute changes far better than a spreadsheet or a general platform's seating add-on.

Will attendees actually use the event app?

Most will if it holds the agenda, maps, and their meetings in one place. Promote it before the event, and make check-in or session access depend on it, so there is a reason to open it.

How much do event-planning tools cost?

It ranges widely: small tools start at tens of dollars a month, while conference platforms charge per event and attendee count, and enterprise platforms like Cvent are quoted. Always get a quote for your exact attendee numbers before budgeting.

Can one tool run my whole event?

For conferences, Whova or Cvent come closest. For weddings and private events, a combination — Planning Pod plus Social Tables and Zkipster — usually works better.
