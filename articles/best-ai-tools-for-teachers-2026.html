7 Best AI Tools for Teachers in 2026 (Lesson Planning, Grading & Classroom AI)

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read · Category: Education

Teachers spend hours every week on work that never reaches students: writing lesson plans, differentiating worksheets, and marking stacks of quizzes. AI tools built for classrooms now handle much of that prep work — designed around standards, reading levels, and school privacy rules. Here are the seven best tools for teachers in 2026, and which one fits your subject and workload.

Quick Verdict

Short version: MagicSchool AI as the best all-rounder for daily prep, Brisk Teaching for grading and feedback inside Google Docs, Diffit for differentiating any text to any reading level, Curipod for interactive lessons students actually join, Eduaide.AI for feedback and IEP support, Gradescope for fast, consistent grading at scale, and Khanmigo as the safest AI tutor for students.

The 7 Tools, Reviewed

1. MagicSchool AI — Best All-Rounder

MagicSchool AI is the Swiss army knife of teacher AI: more than 60 tools for lesson plans, unit outlines, multiple-choice questions, rubrics, IEP goal drafts, parent emails, and behavior plans.

Free plan: Generous free tier for individual teachers; school and district plans add admin controls and data protections.

Strengths: Huge tool library, built for education standards, strong free tier.

Best for: Teachers who want one tool that covers most of their weekly prep.

Weakness: Output quality varies by tool — always review lesson content for accuracy before teaching it.

2. Brisk Teaching — Best for Grading and Feedback

Brisk lives inside Google Docs and the Chrome browser, where teachers already work. It grades student writing against your rubric, generates targeted feedback comments, adjusts reading levels of any web page, and even detects AI-written student work.

Free plan: Free core features for educators; premium adds deeper feedback and admin tools.

Strengths: Works where you already grade, rubric-based feedback, reading-level adjustment.

Best for: English, history, and any subject with regular written work.

Weakness: Google Classroom and Chrome-centric — less useful outside the Google ecosystem.

3. Diffit — Best for Differentiating Reading Material

Diffit solves one specific, painful problem: the same article is too hard for some students and too easy for others. Paste any text, link, or topic, and Diffit rewrites it at the reading level you choose, then generates a summary, vocabulary list, and comprehension questions to match.

Free plan: Free tier with daily limits; premium (~$10/month) removes limits and adds exports.

Strengths: Genuinely useful differentiation, instant questions and vocabulary, simple interface.

Best for: Mixed-ability classrooms and ESL/EFL teaching.

Weakness: Focused on reading material — it does not plan full lessons or grade work.

4. Curipod — Best for Interactive Lessons

Curipod turns a topic into an interactive lesson in minutes: slides, polls, open questions, word clouds, and drawings, all running live while students join from their devices. AI drafts the lesson and generates discussion prompts.

Free plan: Free tier for trying lessons; school plans unlock full libraries and analytics.

Strengths: Students participate instead of watching, quick lesson generation, built-in engagement tools.

Best for: Whole-class teaching and revision sessions where attention usually drops.

Weakness: Needs student devices and decent internet; less useful for homework or self-study.

5. Eduaide.AI — Best for Feedback and IEP Support

Eduaide.AI focuses on the feedback side of teaching, and includes tools for drafting IEP goals and accommodations. Its "Feedback Assistant" is a standout — paste student work, and it suggests specific, constructive comments tied to your criteria.

Free plan: Free tier with limited generations; pro (~$6/month) for unlimited use.

Strengths: Strong feedback tools, IEP and special-education support, affordable.

Best for: Teachers handling large classes or special-education paperwork.

Weakness: Smaller tool library than MagicSchool; interface feels utilitarian.

6. Gradescope — Best for Grading at Scale

Gradescope is built for high-volume, consistent grading: scan paper exams or import digital submissions, build a rubric once, and grade similar answers in batches. Its AI groups similar responses together, so you judge each answer type once instead of re-reading the same mistake fifty times.

Free plan: Free for individual instructors on basic use; institutional plans add full features and integrations.

Strengths: Fastest grading for large classes, consistent rubric application, works with paper and digital work.

Best for: Maths, science, and exam-heavy courses with large cohorts.

Weakness: Overkill for small classes; setup takes time the first time you build a rubric.

7. Khanmigo — Best AI Tutor for Students

Khanmigo, from Khan Academy, is the AI tutor designed to be safe for students: it guides learners with Socratic questions instead of handing over answers, and it gives teachers a dashboard view of student conversations. Where general chatbots raise cheating concerns, Khanmigo is built to teach rather than tell.

Free plan: Free for teachers in the US; student access varies by school and district plan.

Strengths: Tutoring that teaches instead of answering, teacher oversight, trusted Khan Academy content.

Best for: Schools that want students to use AI directly, with guardrails.

Weakness: Availability and pricing vary by region; less of a general prep tool than MagicSchool.

Which One Should You Pick?

Starting out? MagicSchool AI covers the most ground for free, and you will feel the time saving within a week. Drowning in marking? Brisk Teaching for written work, Gradescope for exams and large classes. Mixed-ability class? Diffit is the single most useful differentiation tool on this list. Want students to use AI safely? Khanmigo is the only option here designed for student-facing tutoring with teacher oversight.

Tips for Using AI in the Classroom

Always check facts before teaching them. AI lesson content can include plausible-sounding errors — treat every draft as a first draft. Keep student data private. Never paste names or grades into tools your school has not approved. Start with one workflow. Pick a single pain point — lesson plans, or grading — and master one tool for it before adding more.

FAQ

Are AI tools for teachers free?

Most tools on this list have a genuinely useful free tier for individual teachers. Schools usually pay only when they want admin controls, district-wide data protections, or unlimited generations.

Is it safe to put student work into AI tools?

Only use tools your school has approved, and avoid entering student names or identifying details. Tools built for education typically offer stronger privacy terms than general chatbots.

Which AI tool is best for lesson planning?

MagicSchool AI, for its breadth of planning tools. For interactive, student-participation lessons, Curipod is the better pick.

Can AI grade essays fairly?

AI grading works well as a first pass against a clear rubric, especially for formative feedback. For high-stakes or final grades, a teacher should always review and confirm the result.
