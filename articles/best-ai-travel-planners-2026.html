7 Best AI Travel Planners in 2026 (Plan Your Perfect Trip)

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read · Category: AI Lifestyle

Planning a trip used to mean forty open tabs: flights on one site, hotels on another, Reddit threads for "hidden gems," and a spreadsheet holding it together. AI travel planners compress that into a conversation — describe the trip you want and get a day-by-day itinerary with places to stay, eat, and explore, often with booking links. Seven of the best compared — GuideGeek, TripGenie, Mindtrip, Curiosio, Roam Around, Vacay and Wonderplan — so you can plan your next trip in minutes, not weeks.

What to look for in an AI travel planner

  • Itinerary depth: realistic day-by-day schedules with timing, not just a list of landmarks.
  • Live data: actual hotel prices, opening hours, and flight options beat recited Wikipedia facts.
  • Booking integration: can it actually book, or does it just hand you links?
  • Personalization: it should ask about budget, pace, food, and travel style — not ignore them.
  • Offline access: saved itineraries that work with spotty roaming data matter.

1. GuideGeek

From Matador Network, GuideGeek is the most conversational tool here: it lives inside WhatsApp and Instagram DMs, so there's nothing to download. Describe your destination, dates, budget, and interests, and it builds day-by-day itineraries, recommends hidden gems, and answers follow-up questions mid-trip. Completely free, and it remembers your travel style across conversations. Drawback: no map view or booking checkout — it's advice, not a booking engine. Best for quick, personalized answers without learning a new app.

2. Trip.com TripGenie

Trip.com's in-app AI assistant closes the loop between planning and paying: ask for a 5-day Tokyo trip under $1,500 and it returns an itinerary with hotels and flights you can book on the spot at live prices. It handles flight changes, hotel comparisons, and attraction tickets in the same chat. Free inside the Trip.com app. Drawback: it funnels you toward Trip.com's own inventory, so cheaper options elsewhere may be missed. Best for planning and booking together with real prices.

3. Mindtrip

Mindtrip feels like a travel magazine crossed with a planning app: type a trip idea ("4 days in Lisbon, foodie, mid-range budget") and it generates a visual, photo-rich itinerary with maps and restaurant picks — then drag to rearrange days and collaborate with travel companions. Free plan covers basic trip generation; premium tiers start around $15 per month. Drawback: booking happens through partner links, not in-app. Best for visual planners who want a beautiful, shareable itinerary.

4. Curiosio

Curiosio (from the Daytrip team) is built for road trips: enter start and end points and it maps an optimized route with scenic stops, drive times, and a day-by-day breakdown. It excels at logistics other planners fudge — realistic driving durations, fuel/charging stops, sensible overnight breaks. Free to use. Drawback: thinner for city walking itineraries. Best for road-trippers and multi-city route planners.

5. Roam Around

Roam Around takes destination, dates, budget, and interests and outputs a full day-by-day itinerary in seconds, with Google Maps links for every stop. A limited free tier covers a few trips; paid plans start around $10 per month. Drawback: itineraries can feel template-driven on repeat use. Best for anyone who wants a solid plan in under a minute.

6. Vacay

Vacay acts as your AI travel designer: a short quiz on travel style, budget, and must-haves produces polished itineraries — curated restaurants, boutique hotels, experiences matched to your traveler type. It saves your profile so each trip starts smarter than the last, and trip boards share easily. Free basic tier; premium starts around $8-12 per month. Best for style-conscious travelers who want curation over raw logistics.

7. Wonderplan

Wonderplan's pitch is the five-second trip plan: type a destination and instantly get day-by-day activities, hotel and restaurant recommendations, estimated costs, and packing suggestions. Its budget estimator breaks down likely costs per category so you know what the trip will cost before committing. Free with generous usage, plus affordable paid tiers. Drawback: less personalization depth than Mindtrip or Vacay. Best for budget-conscious planners wanting a fast first draft.

Which one should you pick?

  • Want to plan inside a chat you already use: GuideGeek — no app, no account, just message it.
  • Want planning AND booking together: Trip.com TripGenie — the only one here with real-time bookable prices.
  • Planning a road trip: Curiosio — route logic beats every generalist here.
  • Traveling with friends: Mindtrip — collaborative, visual, easy to share.
  • Need a plan right now: Roam Around or Wonderplan — seconds to a full plan.
  • On a strict budget: Wonderplan's cost estimator, then verify prices with TripGenie or a booking site.

One caution: verify anything time-sensitive — opening hours, prices, seasonal closures — before you travel. AI planners occasionally invent restaurants or quote outdated prices.

FAQ

Are AI travel planners free?

Mostly yes, with limits. GuideGeek, TripGenie, and Curiosio are free; Mindtrip, Roam Around, Vacay, and Wonderplan have free tiers with paid upgrades for unlimited trips and deeper personalization.

Can AI travel planners actually book flights and hotels?

Trip.com TripGenie can — it books directly from live inventory. The others generate itineraries and hand you booking links; you complete the purchase on the hotel or airline site.

How accurate are AI-generated itineraries?

Structurally excellent, but details drift: verify opening hours, prices, and whether that "hidden gem" restaurant still exists before you go.

Which AI travel planner is best for a road trip?

Curiosio — purpose-built for multi-stop routes with realistic drive times, which generalist planners handle poorly.

Do these work offline while traveling?

Most need internet to generate plans. Save your finished itinerary as a PDF or screenshot, and export stops to Google Maps, so you have everything with spotty roaming data.

Is it safe to share my travel dates with an AI planner?

Reasonably — these are mainstream consumer tools. Still, avoid pasting passport numbers or exact home addresses, and check each tool's privacy policy if you're cautious.

AI travel planners won't replace the joy of discovering a place yourself, but they've killed the worst part of travel — the tab-hoarding research phase. Pick one that matches your style, sanity-check the details, and enjoy the trip.
