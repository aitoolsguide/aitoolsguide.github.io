7 Best AI Spreadsheet Tools in 2026 (Excel, Sheets & Data Analysis)

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read · Category: Productivity

Spreadsheets run most small businesses, yet most people use only a fraction of what Excel and Google Sheets can do. The gap is rarely effort — it is knowing the right formula, cleaning messy data, or turning a table of numbers into a chart that answers a question. AI spreadsheet tools close that gap: describe what you want in plain English, and the tool writes the formula, cleans the data, or builds the analysis.

Quick Verdict

Short version: Copilot in Excel if your work lives in Microsoft 365; Gemini in Sheets if it lives in Google Workspace; Rows for a modern spreadsheet with AI built in; Formula Bot for generating and explaining formulas; SheetAI for AI inside Google Sheets; Julius AI for chatting with your data and getting charts; and Quadratic for analysts who want Python and AI in the same grid.

The 7 Tools, Reviewed

1. Copilot in Excel — Best for Microsoft 365 Users

Copilot brings an AI assistant directly into the spreadsheet most offices already use. Ask it in plain English to highlight trends, add a formula column, sort and filter data, or explain what a column of numbers means, and it performs the action in the sheet rather than just describing it. It can also suggest a chart that fits the data. Because it works inside Excel and respects Microsoft 365 permissions, it fits teams that cannot move their data to a new tool.

Free plan: No free tier; paid add-on around $30/user/month. Best for: Office workers, accountants, and analysts already in Microsoft 365. Weakness: Hard to justify for occasional users, and it needs a Microsoft 365 subscription underneath.

2. Gemini in Sheets — Best for Google Workspace Users

Gemini in Google Sheets is the Google equivalent: an AI sidebar that helps you build tables, write formulas, summarise data, and generate content such as project plans or budgets from a short prompt. Its "Help me organize" feature turns a plain description — say, a content calendar — into a structured sheet with sensible columns. It can also explain a formula in plain language, which is genuinely useful when you inherit someone else's spreadsheet.

Free plan: No standalone free tier; Gemini features come with Google Workspace or Google AI plans. Best for: Small teams, teachers, and freelancers who plan everything in Google Sheets. Weakness: Less powerful for heavy analysis than dedicated data tools.

3. Rows — Best Modern Spreadsheet with AI Built In

Rows is a spreadsheet rebuilt for the web, with AI woven through it rather than bolted on. Its AI analyst answers questions about your table, generates formulas, enriches data — turning company names into domains and descriptions, for example — and connects directly to tools like Stripe, Salesforce, and Google Analytics. It suits startups and marketing teams who want a spreadsheet that behaves like a lightweight database and reporting tool, not just a grid of cells.

Free plan: Free tier available; paid plans from around $15/user/month. Best for: Startups, marketers, and operations teams who want analysis and integrations without leaving the sheet. Weakness: A smaller ecosystem than Excel or Sheets, so colleagues may need convincing to switch.

4. Formula Bot — Best for Generating and Explaining Formulas

Formula Bot does one job extremely well: it turns plain English into spreadsheet formulas, and turns cryptic formulas back into plain English. Type "calculate commission at 8% for sales over 10,000" and it returns a working formula for Excel or Google Sheets, with an explanation of each part. It also includes data cleaning and templates. If formulas are your bottleneck, this is the cheapest fix on this list.

Free plan: Free tier with limited generations; paid plans from around $9/month. Best for: Anyone who regularly gets stuck on formulas — finance staff, shop owners, and students included. Weakness: It is a helper, not a full spreadsheet; you still work in Excel or Sheets.

5. SheetAI — Best AI Add-On Inside Google Sheets

SheetAI is an add-on that puts AI functions directly into Google Sheets cells. Instead of copying data to a chatbot, you write a formula that categorises, translates, or summarises text, and the result appears in the sheet, row by row. That makes it powerful for repetitive text work: classifying feedback, translating product descriptions, or generating product copy at scale — hundreds of rows at a time, without manual copy-and-paste.

Free plan: Free tier with limited credits; paid plans add more credits. Best for: E-commerce sellers, marketers, and support teams processing large lists inside Sheets. Weakness: Credit-based pricing can add up on very large sheets.

6. Julius AI — Best for Chatting with Your Data

Julius AI takes a different approach: upload a spreadsheet or CSV, then ask questions in plain English — "Which product grew fastest last quarter?" — and get back an answer, a chart, or a cleaned dataset you can download. It can run statistical analysis, build visualisations, and explain its reasoning step by step, which makes it useful for people who need analysis but do not write code. Always verify its numbers against your source data before presenting them.

Free plan: Free tier with limited analyses; paid plans from around $20/month. Best for: Non-technical users who need real analysis and charts from a spreadsheet, fast. Weakness: Uploading data raises privacy questions — avoid sensitive data unless you have checked its terms.

7. Quadratic — Best for Analysts Who Want Python in a Spreadsheet

Quadratic is a spreadsheet for people who outgrew formulas: it puts Python, SQL, and AI in the same grid, so a cell can hold a Python dataframe or a database query instead of just a number. Its AI assistant writes Python code for analysis, explains a formula, or generates charts from your data. Data teams use it for work too complex for Excel but too small for a full data pipeline. It is more technical than everything else here, but it removes the constant exporting between a spreadsheet and a notebook.

Free plan: Free tier available; paid plans add team features and more AI usage. Best for: Data analysts and technical teams who already use Python. Weakness: Overkill for simple budgets; non-technical users will find it intimidating.

Which One Should You Pick?

Already in Excel or Sheets? Start with the AI built into the tool you use — Copilot or Gemini — before adding anything new. Stuck on formulas? Formula Bot solves the specific problem for a few dollars. Working with lists of text? SheetAI processes hundreds of rows inside Sheets. Need charts and answers, not formulas? Julius AI is the fastest route from raw data to insight. Doing serious analysis? Rows suits teams, Quadratic suits analysts who code.

Tips for Using AI in Spreadsheets

Keep a copy of the original data. AI can overwrite, mis-sort, or hallucinate a value; work on a copy and keep the source untouched. Verify every formula. Test an AI-generated formula on a few rows you can check by hand before dragging it across ten thousand. Be specific in prompts. Name the columns, the range, and the exact result you want. Protect sensitive data. Do not upload payroll, customer, or medical data without checking privacy and retention terms. Note that prices change often — check each tool's current pricing page before committing.

FAQ

Can AI replace Excel skills?

No — it lowers the barrier, but you still need to judge whether an answer is plausible. The most valuable skill is now checking AI output: spotting a wrong total, a misread date, or a formula that works on the example but fails on edge cases.

Is my spreadsheet data private when I use an AI tool?

It depends on the tool. Built-in assistants from Microsoft and Google operate under your existing workspace terms, while standalone tools vary. Read the privacy policy, avoid uploading sensitive data where possible, and prefer tools that state clearly that your data is not used for training.

Which AI spreadsheet tool is free?

Rows, Formula Bot, SheetAI, Julius AI, and Quadratic all offer free tiers with limits. The free tiers are enough to test whether a tool fits your workflow, though regular heavy use will usually need a paid plan.

Can AI build a whole spreadsheet for me?

Yes, for common cases. Gemini's "Help me organize" and Copilot can generate a structured budget, tracker, or plan from a description. Review the columns and categories before use — the structure is usually good, but the details reflect your prompt, not your business.

Do AI spreadsheet tools work with my existing Excel files?

Most do. Copilot works inside Excel itself, Formula Bot generates Excel formulas, and Julius AI and Quadratic accept Excel and CSV uploads. Google-focused tools like Gemini and SheetAI need your file in Google Sheets, which takes one import step.
