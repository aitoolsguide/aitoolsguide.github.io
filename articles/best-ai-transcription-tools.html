Best AI Transcription Tools: Audio to Text in 2026

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read · Category: AI Audio

Turning audio into text is one of the most useful things AI can do right now — captions, show notes, meeting notes. Modern AI transcription is fast, cheap, and roughly 90–98% accurate on clear audio. But tools differ wildly in pricing, free limits, language support, and purpose. We compared seven of the biggest names on accuracy, free minutes, and real-world fit.

Quick Verdict

Short version: Otter.ai for free meeting notes, Descript if you edit podcasts or video, Rev when accuracy is critical, Whisper for free and private batch work, Notta for multilingual value, Fireflies.ai for sales teams, and Sonix for multilingual subtitles.

The 7 Tools, Reviewed

1. Otter.ai — Best for Meeting Notes

Otter's AI notetaker joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams calls and transcribes in real time, identifying speakers and generating summaries with action items. Roughly 90–95% accuracy on clean meeting audio — good for notes, not for publishing verbatim.

Free plan: 300 minutes/month (30-minute cap per conversation). Paid from ~$8–9/month billed annually.

Languages: Primarily English-focused.

Best for: Freelancers and teams who want notes that write themselves.

Weakness: Built for live meetings — caption export feels secondary.

2. Rev — Best When Accuracy Can't Slip

Rev offers AI transcription at a few cents per minute, and human transcriptionists at roughly $1.50–1.99 per minute with a 99%+ accuracy claim — the standard for legal and publishing workflows.

Free plan: Around 45 AI transcription minutes per month. This is a paid tool in practice.

Languages: 36+ languages; English is the strongest.

Best for: Legal and investigative work, journalists publishing verbatim interviews, and anyone who needs a court-grade transcript.

Weakness: No real editing environment; the free tier is too small for regular use.

3. Descript — Best for Podcasters and Video Creators

Descript is a video and podcast editor with excellent transcription built in: upload media, get a ~95%-accurate transcript, then edit the audio by editing the text. One click strips filler words, and Overdub replaces words in your own AI voice.

Free plan: 60 media minutes/month. Paid from ~$16/month billed annually.

Languages: 23–25 languages; English most reliable.

Best for: Podcasters and YouTubers who want transcription as part of editing.

Weakness: Limited free minutes; not a meeting notetaker.

4. Notta — Best Multilingual Value

Notta supports 58 languages, claims up to ~98.86% accuracy, and includes live meeting transcription like Otter — for roughly $8/month, noticeably cheaper than the big three, plus real-time translation for cross-language interviews.

Free plan: Small monthly allowance — enough to test, not to rely on.

Languages: 58 including Hindi and Urdu.

Best for: Multilingual teams and Hindi/Urdu creators who want Otter-style features at a lower price.

Weakness: Fewer integrations than the big three.

5. Whisper (OpenAI) — Best Free and Private Option

Whisper is the open-source model that changed this market. Large-v3 supports 99 languages — including Hindi and Urdu — with excellent accuracy on clear audio, at zero cost if you run it yourself: no minute limit, no upload, and your audio never leaves your machine.

Free plan: Fully free self-hosted. The catch: you need a decent GPU (slow on a CPU) and some command-line comfort; free wrappers like Subtitle Edit and MacWhisper help.

Languages: 99 languages with auto-detection; strong Hindi/Urdu performance.

Best for: YouTube captions on a budget, sensitive interviews, and developers building transcription into products.

Weakness: No interface, speaker labeling, or meeting integration — you assemble it yourself.

6. Fireflies.ai — Best for Sales Teams

Fireflies.ai is a meeting notetaker aimed at sales teams. Like Otter, it joins calls and transcribes in real time, but its standout is CRM integration: transcripts, summaries, and action items flow into HubSpot, Salesforce, and similar tools automatically.

Free plan: Small transcription credit; paid seats run around $10/month billed annually.

Languages: 100+ languages — a genuine strength for international teams.

Best for: Sales teams, agencies, and recruiters who want transcripts that feed directly into their CRM.

Weakness: Built for team workflows, not individual creators.

7. Sonix — Best Multilingual Subtitle Workflow

Sonix is the strongest pick when your work spans languages: 49+ languages with auto-detection, 94–96% reported accuracy on clean English audio, and automated translation plus subtitling in one workflow — including trained Urdu support with speaker labels and 18+ export formats.

Free plan: Trial only (~30 minutes); paid starts around $22/month or ~$10/hour pay-as-you-go.

Languages: 49+ languages including Urdu and Hindi with auto-detection.

Best for: Content teams publishing in multiple languages, and creators needing Urdu/Hindi transcription plus captions in one workflow.

Weakness: No live meeting mode, no real free tier.

Which One Should You Pick?

  • YouTube captions on a budget: Whisper via Subtitle Edit, or Descript if you edit there anyway.
  • Meeting notes: Otter.ai for the free tier; Fireflies.ai if you live in a CRM.
  • Podcasts: Descript — the transcript is the editing process.
  • Urdu or Hindi content: Notta for the affordable plan, Sonix for the pro caption workflow, Whisper for free.
  • Must-be-perfect transcripts: Rev's human tier — and proofread even that.

FAQ

Is AI transcription as accurate as human transcription?

On clear, single-speaker audio, the best AI tools hit 94–98% — good enough for captions, notes, and drafts. AI still stumbles on heavy accents, overlapping speech, and noisy rooms. Rev's human service claims 99%+ and remains the standard for legal or publishing work. Use AI for everything; have a human review anything that goes to print.

What's the best free transcription option?

Otter.ai's 300 free minutes per month is the most generous cloud option. Whisper run locally has no minute caps at all — it just needs some technical setup.

Which tools support Urdu and Hindi?

Whisper supports both and works offline — the best free pick. Among paid tools, Notta covers 58 languages including Hindi and Urdu at a low price, and Sonix offers 49+ languages with trained Urdu support. Otter.ai and Rev are primarily English-focused.

Which tool is best for YouTube captions?

Whisper (via Subtitle Edit or MacWhisper) produces quality SRT/VTT files for free, and YouTube Studio's auto-captions are a fine starting point. If you edit in Descript, its transcript-to-caption workflow is the smoothest; Sonix is worth it for multilingual subtitles.

Are my uploads private?

Rev, Sonix, and most paid tiers advertise SOC 2 compliance and encryption. But cloud services still receive your audio. If privacy is non-negotiable — legal clients, unreleased content, sensitive interviews — run Whisper locally so nothing leaves your machine.
