8 Best AI Tools for LinkedIn Creators in 2026 (Posts, Carousels & Growth)

Updated November 2026 · 6 min read · Category: Creators

LinkedIn is the one social network where a single good post can bring you clients, job offers, and a reputation. But the platform rewards consistency and quality at the same time, and writing a sharp post every day is a job in itself. The best AI tools in 2026 help you find ideas, draft in your voice, format posts that get read, design carousels, and measure what works. Here are eight tools creators use.

Quick Verdict

Taplio for all-in-one growth, AuthoredUp for formatting and scheduling, Shield for analytics, ChatGPT for ideas and drafts, Claude for long-form posts, Canva for carousels, Supergrow for ideas and scheduling, and ContentIn for turning notes into posts.

The 8 Tools, Reviewed

1. Taplio — Best All-in-One LinkedIn Growth Tool

Taplio is the closest thing to a complete LinkedIn toolkit. Its AI writes posts from a short prompt, suggests viral ideas based on what works in your niche, and builds a content calendar. It also includes lead-finding features and a simple CRM for tracking conversations. Always rewrite the hook yourself.

Best for: AI writing, post ideas, scheduling, leads. Watch out for: drafts can sound generic — edit every post.

2. AuthoredUp — Best for Formatting and Scheduling

AuthoredUp does two things very well: it makes your posts look good and publishes them on schedule. Its editor previews exactly how a post will appear on LinkedIn, with formatting, spacing control, and a readability score. You can save hooks and templates, draft in bulk, and schedule a week of content.

Best for: formatting, preview, scheduling, hook templates. Watch out for: it does not write for you — pair it with ChatGPT or Claude.

3. Shield Analytics — Best LinkedIn Analytics

LinkedIn's built-in analytics are thin, and guessing which posts work is a bad strategy. Shield gives you real data: follower growth, engagement by post type, and best posting times. It breaks down impressions, likes, comments, and shares per post. The insight is usually simple — carousels beat text posts for you, or failure stories get twice the comments of tips.

Best for: analytics, best posting times, content insights. Watch out for: data only helps if you review it weekly.

4. ChatGPT — Best for Ideas and First Drafts

ChatGPT is the fastest way to go from a blank page to a usable first draft. Ask it for ten hooks or five post angles on your topic. It also repurposes long content — a blog post, a talk, a case study — into a week's worth of LinkedIn posts. Feed it your best posts and ask it to match that style, and the output improves dramatically.

Best for: ideas, hooks, repurposing, first drafts. Watch out for: ban generic corporate phrases from your prompts.

5. Claude — Best for Thoughtful Long-Form Posts

When a post needs to sound like you actually thought about it, Claude is the stronger writer. It handles longer, nuanced drafts — a lesson learned, a contrarian take, a detailed how-to — without slipping into a robotic tone. Give it examples of your writing, a clear point of view, and the story behind the post, and it produces drafts that need light editing rather than a rewrite.

Best for: long-form posts, nuanced takes, matching your voice. Watch out for: slower than ChatGPT for quick idea lists.

6. Canva — Best for Carousels and Visuals

Carousels are LinkedIn's highest-engagement format, and Canva is the easiest way to make them. Its carousel templates, brand kits, and AI image tools turn a post's key points into a clean, swipeable deck in under an hour. Keep each slide to one idea and very few words — dense slides get swiped past, not saved.

Best for: carousels, banners, quote graphics, brand consistency. Watch out for: keep slides minimal — one idea per slide.

7. Supergrow — Best for AI Content Ideas and Scheduling

Supergrow combines AI idea generation with scheduling in a tool built specifically for LinkedIn. Tell it your niche and it generates post ideas, drafts, and carousel outlines, then lets you schedule them in a visual calendar. Its swipe file of proven post formats helps you try structures that work — list posts, story posts, contrarian takes. It is lighter and cheaper than Taplio.

Best for: AI ideas, carousel outlines, scheduling, post formats. Watch out for: your real stories beat generic AI advice.

8. ContentIn — Best for Turning Notes into Posts

ContentIn is built around a simple idea: your best LinkedIn posts already exist in your notes, articles, and conversations. It takes long-form content — a blog post, a newsletter, a meeting summary — and turns it into a series of LinkedIn posts. It also offers AI writing, scheduling, and a library of post templates. For consultants and founders who produce long content, it is the fastest way to feed LinkedIn.

Best for: repurposing articles and notes, templates, scheduling. Watch out for: add a personal line or a real number to every post.

A Weekly LinkedIn Workflow

On Monday, spend 30 minutes in ChatGPT and Supergrow generating ideas. Draft three posts in Claude and two carousels in Canva. On Tuesday, format everything in AuthoredUp, schedule the week, and write one post from a real experience — your own story usually beats the AI drafts. Post daily, reply to comments, and review Shield on Friday. That loop turns LinkedIn from a chore into a channel that brings opportunities to you.

FAQ

Can AI write my LinkedIn posts for me?

AI can draft them, but should not publish them untouched. The best posts carry your stories, opinions, and numbers — use AI for structure and first drafts, then add the human part.

Will LinkedIn penalise AI-generated content?

LinkedIn does not ban AI-assisted writing, but its algorithm rewards genuine engagement. Posts that read like generic AI text get ignored by readers, which hurts reach.

How often should I post on LinkedIn?

Three to five times a week is the sweet spot. Daily posting works if quality stays high, but one strong post beats five forgettable ones.

Are carousels really better than text posts?

On average, carousels earn more saves and shares. But a great text post with a strong story can outperform a mediocre carousel — format matters less than the idea.

Which tool should I start with if I can only pay for one?

Taplio for growth and writing in one place, or AuthoredUp if you already write well and just need formatting and scheduling. Pair either with the free tiers of ChatGPT and Canva.
