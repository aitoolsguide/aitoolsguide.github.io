7 Best AI Meeting Assistants in 2026 (Never Take Notes Again)

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read · Category: AI Productivity

Trying to take notes and listen in a meeting is a losing trade. AI meeting assistants fix it: they capture your calls, transcribe them, and hand you action items and a summary afterwards. Seven widely used tools compared — Otter.ai, Fireflies.ai, Fathom, tl;dv, Read AI, Krisp and Granola — so you can pick the one that fits how you meet.

What to look for in an AI meeting assistant

  • Bot vs. botless: Most tools send a visible bot into the call; some record on your device instead.
  • Transcription accuracy: Accents, jargon, and crosstalk still break every engine.
  • Summary quality: You need decisions, owners, and deadlines extracted — not filler.
  • Privacy and consent: Where is audio stored, and who can access it?

1. Otter.ai

The original mainstream notetaker and still the strongest for live transcription: the transcript scrolls during the call, so you can catch missed points without asking. Covers Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, with mobile apps and Salesforce/HubSpot sync. Free tier: 300 transcription minutes a month, 30-minute cap per conversation. Drawback: accuracy lags newer rivals in reviews, and the bot joins as a visible participant.

2. Fireflies.ai

The depth pick: a bot-based recorder for Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams with the widest integration spread here — 100+ integrations with native CRM autofill, 100+ languages with auto-detection, an API, and built-in AI skills for follow-ups. Free tier: unlimited transcription, but limited storage (about 400 minutes pooled per team) and few AI summaries. Drawback: the free tier behaves like a trial. Paid plans from about $10 per seat per month billed annually — check current pricing.

3. Fathom

Wins the free-tier contest: unlimited recordings, transcription, and storage at $0, with advanced AI summaries capped at five calls monthly. Summaries are genuinely useful — action items, timestamps, one-click clip sharing — and paid tiers add sales templates, Deal View, and Salesforce/HubSpot sync. Covers Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams. Drawback: automation is thinner than Fireflies. Paid plans around $16 per user per month billed annually — check current pricing.

4. tl;dv

Built for async teams: records and transcribes, then lets you timestamp and share short clips — so a teammate who missed the call watches the two minutes that matter. Works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, supports 30+ languages, connects to thousands of apps via Zapier. Free plan: unlimited recordings and transcription kept about three months, full AI notes on your first 10 meetings. Drawback: recurring AI summaries and longer retention need the paid tier (about $18 per seat per month billed annually).

5. Read AI

The measurement play: alongside summaries for Zoom, Meet, and Teams, it scores meetings — engagement, sentiment, speaker balance — and generates coaching reports. Useful for sales managers watching call behavior, with Salesforce/HubSpot sync on paid tiers. Drawback: the free plan covers only about five meetings a month, and without the analytics you're paying for what others give away free. Paid plans from around $15 per user per month — check current pricing.

6. Krisp

The odd one out: it started as noise cancellation and is still the best tool here at making the meeting itself sound better. Device-level capture — no bot, works in any app, records in-person conversations — with two-sided noise cancellation and accent conversion. In noisy rooms, nothing else here helps as much. Drawback: no permanent free tier, only a roughly 7-day trial, and notes are secondary to audio processing. Core around $8 per user per month on annual billing — check current pricing.

7. Granola

For people who take their own notes and want AI to sharpen them: it records on your device (never a bot on the call), then blends your typed jottings with what it heard into clean notes that read like you wrote them. Mac and Windows apps, iOS and Android support, and Notion/HubSpot/Zapier integrations on the paid tier. Caveats: free Basic keeps only 30 days of history — fatal for an archive — and it stores notes, not audio or video, so you can't replay a disputed moment. Business around $14 per user per month.

Which one should you pick?

  • Sales teams: Fathom for free capture plus CRM sync and Deal View; Read AI for call-coaching analytics too.
  • Solo freelancers: Fathom's free tier covers nearly everything; tl;dv if you share clips with clients.
  • Privacy-sensitive meetings: Granola or Krisp capture locally with no bot — but announcing the recording is still on you.
  • Google Meet-only users: Any tool here works with Meet — decide on the free tier and workflow instead.
  • Best free option: Fathom — unlimited recordings and transcription at $0.

One caution for all of them: AI transcripts are not court records. Verify quotes, numbers, and commitments before sending anything important.

FAQ

Is it legal to record meetings with AI?

It depends on your jurisdiction — many require all-party consent. Announce the recording at the start of every call, and check local law for client calls.

Do I need to tell participants an AI bot is recording?

Yes. Bots announce themselves by joining, but people ignore that. With bot-free tools like Granola or Krisp, explicit disclosure is your responsibility.

Which meeting assistant is most accurate?

No tool wins everywhere — it depends on audio quality, accents, jargon, and crosstalk. Run the same real meeting through two or three free tiers before committing to a plan.

Can AI meeting notes replace a human note-taker?

For status calls, sales calls, and interviews: yes, with a quick human review. For negotiations where nuance matters, AI misses subtext a human catches.

Do these tools store my meeting recordings?

Yes — almost all store recordings and transcripts in the cloud. Check each tool's retention and deletion controls before recording anything confidential.

Which free AI meeting assistant is best?

Fathom: unlimited recordings, transcription, and storage at $0, with only advanced AI summaries capped at five a month. tl;dv is the runner-up.
