What voice cloning is (and the consent rule)

Voice cloning trains an AI model on recordings of a voice, then speaks any typed text in that voice. The technology is neutral — the use is what matters. One hard rule: never clone someone else's voice without their explicit permission. Fraudulent clones are illegal in many jurisdictions, which is why legitimate tools verify the voice is yours during setup. Be suspicious of any service that doesn't.

What to look for in a voice cloning tool

  • Clone quality: The best clones capture pacing, emotion, and breath sounds. Test samples with your own audio.
  • Audio required: Some tools need just a minute of clean speech; professional clones ask for 30 minutes or more.
  • Language support: One language is standard; speaking new languages in your voice varies by tool.
  • Consent safeguards: Legitimate tools verify the voice is yours. If a tool has no verification, steer clear.
  • Commercial use and API: Check commercial usage rights and whether an API exists for apps.

The 7 tools

1. ElevenLabs

The quality benchmark: ElevenLabs' clones are consistently the hardest to distinguish from the original voice, with natural pacing and emotional range in 30+ languages. Instant cloning needs as little as one minute of audio; the professional tier trains on ~30 minutes for studio-grade fidelity. Plans start around $5/mo by character quota. Drawback: high-volume generation gets pricey. Best when the clone must be indistinguishable from you.

2. Descript Overdub

The most practical clone for podcasters: Overdub builds a copy of your voice inside Descript's text-based editor, so when you rewrite a sentence, the clone says the corrected line in your voice — no re-recording, no audible edit. Training means reading scripts (roughly 10-30 minutes of speech), with voice verification during setup. Included in Descript plans from around $12/mo. Drawback: it's for fixing recordings, not generating narration from scratch. If you record with your own voice, this is the clone you'll use daily.

3. PlayHT

Instant cloning from short samples, hundreds of voices, 140+ languages, and a strong API. Free tier for testing; paid plans from around $19/mo. Drawback: emotional delivery trails ElevenLabs, and the cloning workflow feels less focused than specialist tools. Strong pick when you need cloning plus API and wide language coverage in one place.

4. Resemble AI

The ethics-first, developer-friendly option: consent safeguards include a detector that checks whether audio was generated by its own models. Cloning needs about 10 minutes of clean audio, plus real-time voice changing and multilingual generation. Plans from around $19/mo. Drawback: raw quality trails ElevenLabs, and the interface skews developer. Best for businesses that need cloning with verifiable safeguards and API access.

5. Speechify

The accessibility and audiobook pick: articles, PDFs, and books read back in a voice modeled on your own. Cloning requires reading a short sample in the app; premium runs around $11/mo billed annually. Drawback: it's a feature inside a reading app, not a voice studio — no API-grade generation, limited emotional control. Ideal for heavy reading workloads and audiobook-style narration.

6. Coqui XTTS (open source)

The free, run-it-yourself option: an open-source multilingual cloning model that runs on your own hardware, supporting 17 languages from just seconds of reference audio. No subscription, no quotas, no cloud — your voice data never leaves your machine. Drawback: you need a decent GPU and technical comfort, and quality trails the commercial leaders on long emotional passages. The best free cloning tool for technical users who want zero cloud dependency.

7. LOVO

The content-creator studio: LOVO's Genny platform bundles instant cloning with a production workspace — script assistance, 150+ languages, video editing, subtitles. Cloning works from short samples; the 500+ voice library is strong for commercial content. Plans from around $19/mo. Drawback: fidelity doesn't top ElevenLabs, and credits are consumed across all features. Best for creators who want cloning inside an end-to-end video workflow.

Which one should you pick?

  • Best overall: ElevenLabs — the most convincing clone, from a one-minute sample up to studio-grade training.
  • Best for podcasters/video creators: Descript Overdub — it fixes your actual recordings, which is what creators need most.
  • Best free/open source: Coqui XTTS — no cost, no cloud, if you have a GPU and some technical patience.
  • Best for developers: PlayHT or Resemble AI for APIs; Resemble if consent safeguards matter to your product.
  • Best for multilingual content: LOVO or PlayHT for the widest language coverage in your cloned voice.
  • Best for listening, not producing: Speechify — have anything read back in your own voice.

One caution: a clone is only as good as its training audio — record in a quiet room, close to the mic, with varied sentences. And disclose AI-generated voice where your audience expects a human: listeners forgive AI voices, they don't forgive being deceived by them.

FAQ

Is AI voice cloning legal?

Cloning your own voice, or one you have permission to use, is legal. Cloning someone else's voice without consent is illegal in many jurisdictions. Legitimate tools build consent verification into their flow.

How much audio is needed to clone a voice?

Instant cloning typically needs 1-5 minutes of clear speech; professional-grade clones ask for 10-30 minutes. More audio generally means better fidelity, but a clean 3-minute sample beats a noisy 30-minute one.

Can I clone someone else's voice?

Only with explicit permission. Reputable tools verify the voice is yours — usually by having you read a prompted sentence aloud — and some block public figures entirely. A service that clones any voice without questions is a red flag, not a feature.

Do cloned voices work in other languages?

Yes, on the better tools — ElevenLabs, PlayHT, and LOVO can speak other languages in your cloned voice, though accent varies. Expect the most natural result in the training language.

What is the best free voice cloning tool?

Coqui XTTS — open source, runs on your computer, clones from seconds of audio, at the cost of setup effort and a GPU. Among cloud tools, ElevenLabs and PlayHT have free tiers generous enough to test cloning before paying.
