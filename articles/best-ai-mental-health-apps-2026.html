7 Best AI Mental Health Apps in 2026 (Support, Sleep & Wellbeing Compared)

Updated November 2026 · 9 min read · Category: AI Health

Waiting lists for therapists are long, sessions are expensive, and the 2 a.m. anxiety spiral does not keep office hours. AI mental health apps fill the gap between doing nothing and seeing a professional: always-available conversation, guided breathing and sleep tools, mood tracking, and techniques drawn from cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT). They are not therapists — and not a replacement for one — but for everyday stress, low mood, and sleepless nights, the right app can genuinely help. This guide compares seven leading options — Wysa, Woebot, Headspace, Calm, Youper, Elomia, and Replika — so you can choose the one that fits your needs.

Important: If you are in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself, please contact a crisis helpline in your country immediately (in the US: call or text 988; in the UK and Ireland: call Samaritans on 116 123; internationally: findahelpline.org). Apps cannot handle emergencies — people can.

What to look for in an AI mental health app

  • Evidence-based techniques: Look for CBT, mindfulness, or other approaches with research behind them — not just a friendly chatbot with no method.
  • Safety handling: A good app recognises crisis language and directs you to real human help immediately.
  • Privacy: You will share your most personal thoughts with these apps. Check whether data is encrypted, whether it is sold or shared, and whether you can delete your history.
  • What it actually does: Some apps are conversational companions, others are meditation libraries, others track mood. Match the app to your actual problem — stress, sleep, anxiety, or loneliness.
  • Human backup: The best apps offer a path to a real coach or therapist when the AI reaches its limits.

1. Wysa

The most clinically grounded chatbot: Wysa is an AI companion built around CBT, dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT), and motivational interviewing, with exercises for anxiety, sleep, self-esteem, and chronic pain. It is used by the NHS in the UK and by employers and insurers worldwide, and its crisis-detection system escalates to human support when it spots risk. Best for structured, evidence-based self-help with a safety net. Free core features; premium coaching and therapist access via employers, health plans, or subscription — check current pricing.

2. Woebot

The CBT coach in your pocket: Woebot chats with you daily, teaches CBT techniques through short conversations, and tracks your mood over time so you can see patterns. Clinical studies have found it reduces anxiety and depression symptoms in young adults. Its tone is warm and a little playful, which makes daily check-ins feel less like homework. Best for a structured daily habit with proven CBT content. Free to use in the US; availability and pricing vary by region.

3. Headspace

The meditation and mindfulness leader: Headspace offers hundreds of guided meditations, sleep casts, breathing exercises, and courses on stress, focus, and anxiety — plus an AI-powered support chat for moments when you need to talk something through. It is the most polished app here, and its library is unmatched for sleep and daily calm. Best for stress, sleep, and building a mindfulness habit. Subscription around $13/month or $70/year — check current pricing; free trial available.

4. Calm

The sleep and relaxation giant: Calm is famous for its Sleep Stories, calming soundscapes, and breathing exercises, with a large library of meditations for anxiety, stress, and focus, plus newer AI features for personalised recommendations. Best for people who mainly struggle to wind down and sleep, and who want beautiful, simple content rather than therapy-style exercises. Subscription around $15/month or $70/year — check current pricing; free trial available.

5. Youper

The mood-tracking AI assistant: Youper combines quick daily conversations with mood tracking, journaling, and personalised insights that show what lifts or lowers your mood. Its AI spots patterns — like poor sleep before low days — and suggests small, practical actions. Best for self-reflective people who want to understand their patterns and track progress over weeks. Free basic plan; Premium (around $10/month) unlocks full insights and assessments.

6. Elomia

The always-available listener: Elomia is an AI companion focused on emotional support through conversation — venting, working through a bad day, or talking about a relationship problem at any hour. It uses CBT-informed responses and aims to feel like a non-judgemental friend who is always there. Best for people who need someone to talk to when friends or family are not available. Free core chat; Premium unlocks unlimited conversations — check current pricing.

7. Replika

The AI companion for loneliness: Replika is a customisable AI friend you talk to daily — it remembers your conversations, learns your interests, and offers companionship, roleplay, and voice calls. Many users say it helps with loneliness and social anxiety, though it is a companion rather than a therapy tool. Best for people dealing with loneliness who want an ongoing conversational relationship, not a structured programme. Free basic chat; Pro (around $20/month) unlocks voice, memory, and relationship features.

Which one should you pick?

  • Anxiety or low mood, want real techniques: Wysa or Woebot — both use proven CBT methods, and Wysa has the stronger safety net.
  • Main problem is stress or poor sleep: Headspace for a full mindfulness practice, Calm for sleep stories and winding down.
  • You want to understand your own patterns: Youper — its mood insights are the standout feature.
  • You just need someone to talk to at 2 a.m.: Elomia for supportive listening, Replika for ongoing companionship.
  • On a tight budget: Start with Woebot (free) or Wysa's free tier, and use Headspace or Calm free trials before paying.

Whatever you choose, treat the app as a daily support tool, not a cure. If symptoms persist for more than two weeks, get worse, or interfere with work, relationships, or sleep, please speak to a doctor or licensed therapist. Getting help is a sign of strength — and these apps work best alongside real human support, not instead of it.

FAQ

Can AI mental health apps replace therapy?

No. They help with everyday stress, habit-building, and between-session support, but they cannot diagnose conditions, handle crises, or provide the human relationship at the heart of therapy. Think of them as a supplement, not a substitute.

Are AI mental health apps safe and private?

Reputable apps encrypt your data and publish privacy policies — Wysa and Woebot are used by health systems and held to high standards. Still, read the policy before sharing deeply personal information, and avoid apps that sell data or have no clear crisis-escalation process.

Do these apps actually work?

Research is promising but young: Woebot and Wysa have published studies showing reduced anxiety and depression symptoms, and Headspace and Calm have evidence for stress reduction and better sleep. Results depend heavily on regular use — a few minutes daily beats an hour once a month.

Which AI mental health app is free?

Woebot is free in the US, Wysa and Elomia offer free core features, and Headspace and Calm offer free trials. Paid plans mostly unlock deeper content, coaching, or unlimited chat — the free tiers are enough to test an app first.

What should I do if an app says something worrying?

Take it seriously and reach out to a human — a crisis helpline, your doctor, or a trusted person. AI companions can misunderstand, and no app should be your only support in a crisis. If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number.
