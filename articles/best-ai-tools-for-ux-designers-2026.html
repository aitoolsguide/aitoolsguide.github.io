7 Best AI Tools for UX Designers in 2026 (Wireframes, Research & Testing)

Updated November 2026 · 9 min read · Category: Design

UX design is part craft, part grind: blank-canvas wireframes, hours of interview transcripts, and tests that take weeks to analyze. AI tools speed up the grind without replacing the judgment — generating first-draft screens, turning research notes into themes in minutes, and running unmoderated tests while you sleep. Here are seven tools worth a serious look.

What to look for in an AI UX tool

  • Where it fits your workflow: A tool that exports cleanly to Figma or your design system saves hours; one that traps work in its own format costs them back.
  • First draft, not final answer: AI screens and insights are starting points. The best tools make editing and refining fast, not just generating.
  • Research quality: For testing and analysis tools, check how they handle real users, real data, and privacy — a slick report built on thin data misleads.
  • Real costs: Watch for per-seat pricing, per-test fees, and participant recruitment costs on top of the subscription.

1. Figma AI

Figma AI is the obvious starting point for most UX designers because it lives where the work already happens. It generates first-draft UI from prompts, renames layers, rewrites and translates copy, and summarizes long design files. Because it works inside Figma, AI output lands directly in your real files — no export, no rebuild. Drawback: generated screens still need a designer's eye for spacing, hierarchy, and real content, and some features are limited by plan. AI features are included in paid Figma plans — check current availability for your plan.

2. Uizard

Uizard turns ideas into screens fast: type a prompt, upload a hand-drawn sketch, or paste a screenshot, and it generates editable wireframes and mockups. It is built for speed in the early stages — exploring directions and testing concepts with stakeholders before committing to high-fidelity work. Drawback: output quality suits wireframes and early concepts better than production-ready UI, and you will usually rebuild polished screens in Figma. Pricing is typically a monthly subscription with a free tier.

3. Framer AI

Framer AI goes one step further than mockups: it generates a working, publishable website from a prompt, complete with layout, copy, and responsive behavior. For designers validating a concept or building a landing page, it collapses days of work into an afternoon — and the result is a real site you can test with users. Drawback: it is website-focused, so app flows and complex product UX still belong in Figma, and deep customization needs Framer skills. Pricing is usually a monthly subscription with a free tier.

4. Galileo AI

Galileo AI generates high-fidelity UI designs from text prompts, with an emphasis on polished, modern-looking screens and components. Designers use it to explore visual directions quickly or generate screens for a pitch. Its output tends to look closer to finished design than typical wireframe tools, which makes it useful for stakeholder presentations. Drawback: it is a generation tool, not a full design environment — expect to refine and systematize its output in Figma. Pricing is typically a monthly subscription.

5. Visily

Visily is the wireframing tool for people who do not consider themselves designers: product managers, founders, and UX beginners can turn a prompt, a screenshot, or a sketch into an editable wireframe, then refine it with a simple editor and ready-made templates. For UX teams, it lets non-design stakeholders sketch ideas in a shared format instead of vague descriptions. Drawback: it will not replace Figma for high-fidelity product design, and its templates can feel generic if used unedited. Pricing is usually monthly with a free tier.

6. Maze

Maze handles the testing half of UX: run unmoderated usability tests on prototypes, websites, and live products, then get AI-summarized results — task success rates, heatmaps, misclick analysis, and themes from open-ended answers — without watching hours of sessions. It recruits participants or lets you bring your own, and turns findings into readable reports. Drawback: unmoderated tests miss the "why" behind behavior that a live interview catches, so pair it with moderated research for big decisions. Pricing is typically monthly, with per-response costs for recruited participants.

7. Dovetail

Dovetail is the research repository: store interview transcripts, survey responses, and support tickets in one searchable place, then let AI tag themes, summarize findings, and surface patterns across dozens of sessions. For teams drowning in research nobody revisits, it turns scattered notes into a shared memory searchable by theme or quote. Drawback: its value grows with the volume of research you feed it; a team running one interview a quarter will not see the payoff. Pricing is typically per seat per month.

Which one should you pick?

  • Already working in Figma: Figma AI — it adds speed without changing your workflow.
  • Stuck at the blank canvas: Uizard or Galileo AI for first drafts, depending on whether you want wireframes or polished screens.
  • Need a testable site, fast: Framer AI — a real website beats a mockup for user feedback.
  • Non-designers sketching ideas: Visily keeps early concepts in a format designers can actually use.
  • Testing before launch: Maze for quick unmoderated tests.
  • Research piling up unread: Dovetail — the payoff is a team that actually reuses what users said.

Start with the bottleneck that hurts most — slow first drafts, untested designs, or unread research — and fix that first.

FAQ

Will AI replace UX designers?

No. AI generates drafts and summarizes data, but deciding what problem to solve and what "good" means for real users is still design judgment.

Can I use AI-generated screens in a real product?

As a starting point, yes — but treat them as drafts. Check spacing, accessibility, and consistency with your design system before anything ships.

Is unmoderated testing enough?

For catching usability problems and comparing options, often yes. For understanding why users behave a certain way, or for high-stakes decisions, pair it with moderated interviews.

Do these tools work with my design system?

Figma AI works inside your system directly. Prompt-to-UI tools like Galileo and Uizard generate standalone screens that you adapt to your system — budget time for that step.

How do I keep user research private in AI tools?

Check each tool's data handling: whether your research data trains their models, how long it is retained, and whether enterprise plans offer stronger controls. Remove personal identifiers from transcripts before uploading.
