7 Best AI Grammar Checkers & Proofreading Tools in 2026 (Compared)

Updated November 2026 · 9 min read · Category: AI Writing

A typo in a job application, a muddled sentence in a client email, a clumsy paragraph in a blog post — small writing mistakes cost real credibility. Modern AI grammar checkers do far more than underline spelling errors: they rewrite sentences for clarity, adjust tone, flag passive voice, and catch the subtle mistakes spellcheckers miss. This guide compares seven leading tools — Grammarly, LanguageTool, Hemingway Editor, ProWritingAid, QuillBot, Trinka, and ChatGPT — so you can pick the proofreader that fits how you write.

What to look for in an AI grammar checker

  • Accuracy on real writing: The best tools catch subject-verb errors, wrong words (their/there/they're), and punctuation mistakes without flagging correct sentences as wrong.
  • Style help, not just error-spotting: Rewrites for clarity, tone adjustment, and conciseness matter more than a red underline once your grammar is already decent.
  • Where it works: A browser extension and integrations with Word, Google Docs, Gmail, and your phone keyboard matter more than a standalone editor you never open.
  • Language support: If you write in more than one language — or in English as a second language — multilingual support and ESL-friendly explanations are essential.
  • Privacy: Your drafts pass through these tools' servers. Check data policies before pasting confidential or client work, and use the free tier carefully.

1. Grammarly

The household name, and still the most polished all-rounder: Grammarly checks grammar, spelling, punctuation, clarity, tone, and even plagiarism, everywhere you type — browser extension, Word, Google Docs, mobile keyboard, and desktop app. Its AI rewrites whole sentences, not just single words, and its tone detector warns you when an email sounds harsher than you intended. Best for professionals and students who want one tool that works everywhere. Free plan covers basic corrections; Premium (around $12/month billed annually) unlocks full rewrites, tone, and plagiarism checks — check current pricing.

2. LanguageTool

The multilingual and privacy-friendly alternative: LanguageTool checks grammar and style in 30+ languages, including strong support for French, German, Spanish, and Portuguese, and it is open-source at its core. It catches errors Grammarly sometimes misses in non-English text, and its self-hosted option appeals to privacy-conscious teams and European organisations. Best for multilingual writers and anyone who wants a capable free tool without aggressive upselling. Free plan is genuinely useful; Premium (around $5/month) adds style rewrites and longer text limits.

3. Hemingway Editor

The clarity coach: Hemingway does not obsess over commas — it highlights hard-to-read sentences, passive voice, weak adverbs, and words with simpler alternatives, grading your text for readability. It is the tool that makes your writing shorter, punchier, and easier to read, which is exactly what blog posts, landing pages, and business writing need. Best for writers who already know grammar but want tighter, more readable prose. Free web version available; the desktop app is a one-time purchase (around $20).

4. ProWritingAid

The novelist's proofreader: ProWritingAid goes deeper than any other tool here, with 20+ reports covering overused words, sentence variety, pacing, dialogue tags, sticky sentences, and consistency — the things that matter in long-form fiction and non-fiction. It integrates with Word, Scrivener, and Google Docs. Best for authors, long-form writers, and anyone polishing a book or thesis. Free plan covers basic checks; Premium (around $10/month billed annually, or a lifetime licence) unlocks the full report suite.

5. QuillBot

The paraphrasing specialist: QuillBot's strength is rewriting — paste a clunky sentence and get it rephrased in formal, fluent, creative, or concise modes. It also bundles a grammar checker, summariser, citation generator, and plagiarism checker, making it a favourite with students and researchers who need to reword sources without losing meaning. Best for students and anyone who rewrites a lot. Free plan covers basic paraphrasing and grammar; Premium (around $8/month) unlocks all modes and unlimited use.

6. Trinka

The academic and technical specialist: Trinka is built for academic, scientific, and technical writing — it understands formal tone, corrects subject-specific phrasing, checks journal-style consistency, and even evaluates whether your manuscript matches a target journal's style. Best for researchers, PhD students, and professionals writing papers, grants, or technical reports. Free plan covers basic checks; Premium (around $6/month) unlocks advanced academic corrections and file checks.

7. ChatGPT

The flexible freeform editor: ChatGPT is not a dedicated grammar checker, but paste any text and ask it to proofread, rewrite in a specific tone, shorten, or explain every correction — and it does all of it conversationally. Its strength is custom instructions: "make this sound confident but friendly" or "simplify this for a 12-year-old." Best for anyone who already pays for ChatGPT and wants proofreading on demand without another subscription. Free tier works fine for occasional proofreading; Plus (around $20/month) unlocks the stronger models.

Which one should you pick?

  • One tool for everything, every day: Grammarly — the most complete, most integrated option for general use.
  • You write in multiple languages: LanguageTool — the strongest multilingual checker, with a genuinely useful free plan.
  • Your writing needs to be shorter and clearer: Hemingway Editor — pair it with a grammar checker for the full package.
  • Writing a book, thesis, or long report: ProWritingAid — its deep reports catch what others miss.
  • Student rewriting and summarising sources: QuillBot — built for exactly that workflow.
  • Academic papers or technical documents: Trinka — tuned for formal, technical English.
  • Already paying for ChatGPT: Use it for proofreading first — it handles most everyday needs without another subscription.

A practical tip: run your text through two tools before anything important goes out. Grammarly (or LanguageTool) for errors, then Hemingway for readability. Two passes take two minutes and catch almost everything.

FAQ

Are AI grammar checkers accurate enough to trust?

For everyday writing, yes — the leading tools catch the vast majority of errors. They still make mistakes, especially with names, technical terms, and deliberate style choices, so always review suggestions rather than accepting them all blindly.

Is Grammarly free plan enough?

For basic spelling and grammar, yes. The free plan catches the errors that embarrass you. Premium is worth it if you want sentence rewrites, tone detection, and plagiarism checks — or if you write professionally every day.

Can AI grammar checkers detect plagiarism?

Grammarly Premium, QuillBot Premium, and ProWritingAid include plagiarism checking against web sources. They are useful as a first pass, but they are not a substitute for proper citation or a dedicated academic checker.

Which grammar checker is best for non-native English speakers?

Grammarly and LanguageTool both explain corrections in plain language, which helps you learn. Trinka is also strong for formal academic English. Pick the one whose explanations you actually understand — that is how you improve.

Do these tools store my writing?

Most cloud-based checkers process your text on their servers. Grammarly and LanguageTool state they do not sell your data, and LanguageTool offers self-hosting for maximum privacy. Avoid pasting passwords, private client data, or unpublished manuscripts into any free tool without reading its privacy policy first.
